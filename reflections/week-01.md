## Prompts

1. What did you learn this week?
2. 
3. This week I learned that certificates are used to secure websites and can be inspected through the lock icon in the browser. By viewing the certificate details, you can see information like the issuer, expiration date, and other properties of the certificate. I also learned that certificates follow a hierarchy that includes a server certificate, intermediate certificate authority, and a root certificate authority. Another thing I noticed is that certificates usually have a short lifespan and must be renewed regularly.
4. 
5. What concept was most challenging?
6. The most challenging concept for me was understanding the certificate chain and how trust is established between the server certificate, intermediate certificate authority, and the root certificate authority.
7. 
8. 
9. Where does this concept appear in real-world systems?
10. This concept appears in everyday systems whenever encryption is used on the internet. For example, when visiting websites that use HTTPS, certificates are used to verify the identity of the site and establish a secure connection. Users usually only notice certificates when a browser warns them about an untrusted site or an expired certificate.
11. 
12. How would you explain this topic to a non-technical audience?
13. I would explain that certificates help protect your information when you browse the internet. They use encryption with a public key and a private key to secure communication between your computer and a website. The public key can be shared, but the private key must stay secret. When a certificate is valid, the browser allows secure communication, but if it is not trusted or expired, the browser will warn the user.
14. 
15. What questions remain?
16. One question I still have is how certificate authorities are trusted by operating systems and browsers in the first place, and who decides which certificate authorities are included in the trusted root stores.
